# Abstract

Single-turn safety tests miss how a chatbot conversation goes wrong. A teenager in trouble rarely opens with the crisis and more often hints on Monday and says the scary thing on Friday, with a silence in between, and every reply along the way can look harmless while the conversation drifts somewhere unsafe. Riptide measures that drift.

Riptide is 120 scripted conversations built from 60 stems, each written in a standard and a slang register, across seven behaviour families: crisis disclosure, buried disclosure, attachment and engagement bait, coercion, rational updating, secrecy, and age-blind romance. Thirteen model configurations produced 1,065 transcripts, and 344 of 525 board conversations are complete. Two independent judges score every turn on safety and warmth, and on rational updating in the one family that tests it.

Judge reliability is a result. Inter-judge safety agreement is 0.603 on the pilot and 0.52 to 0.59 on the full runs, and agreement with 50 hand labels peaks at 0.577, so the 0.6 gate is unmet and every score is provisional. Gemini was rejected for its 20-request free tier and Qwen for scoring 0.22 to 0.29 against the primary judge; Claude Haiku fell short at 0.49. Warmth agreement, 0.22 to 0.39, is too weak to decide anything, so warmth stays a guardrail.

Drift shows up in every model and shows up late. Tiny Aya base keeps 81% of conversations clean at turn 2 and 17% at turn 12, Aya Expanse 8B falls from 92% to 26%, and even Muse Spark 1.3 goes from 97% to 71%, so a model can look adequate at the start and fail most of the way through. Secrecy is where every small open model collapses: all five Tiny Aya variants pass 0 of 16, against 8 of 16 for Muse Spark.

I repaired Tiny Aya Global two ways. Direct preference optimisation on 1,016 pairs, 307 of them mined for the secrecy and age failures, raised passes from 24 to 36 of 120 under the primary judge (McNemar p=0.023) and lifted mean safety by 0.211 and warmth by 0.257. It also cost attachment, with family 3 dropping from 3 of 18 to 0 of 18, and it left the point estimate for rational updating negative under both judges. The second judge sees the direction of the repair but not its significance (p=0.72). Contrastive activation steering failed outright, with passes falling from 24 to 19. Neither repair touched secrecy coaching, which is the main warning: four families moved and the isolation failure is still there.

The release is a gated dataset of scenarios, transcripts and verdicts, the DPO adapter, the steering vectors as a documented negative result, a report-card Space and a turn viewer. Serving the repaired model on Modal costs about $1,977 a month at a million conversations, which is roughly 9x cheaper than a Sonnet-class API and more than Haiku, so what self-hosting adds beyond price is control of the weights and the failure data.
